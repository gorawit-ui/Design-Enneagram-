# UX / UI review — measured, 2026-09-10

Requested by the Products Owner after the first deploy: what is missing, what needs adjusting, is it
too empty, and what about type sizes — weighted toward mobile while still working on desktop.

Everything below is measured in a real browser at 390×844 (iPhone) and 1440×960, on the deployed
build, not judged from a screenshot. Where a number is quoted it came from the page.

Two things measured **clean** and are worth saying before the problems: **no horizontal overflow at
any width on any screen**, and inputs are 16px, so iOS will not zoom the page when someone taps a
field. Both are common failures in Thai-language forms and neither is present here.

---

## 1. Type size — the finding as first written was overstated, and here is the corrected version

> **Correction, same day.** The paragraph below originally read "the biggest mobile problem" and
> rested on counting **declarations** in the stylesheet: 119 font sizes, 31 of them under 14px. That
> count is real but it does not measure what a person sees, because most of those declarations are
> dead — overridden by a later layer, or scoped to an element that never renders. Walking every
> rendered text node across all four steps at 390px found **3 nodes under 14px, all three from the
> audience picker added in the same session**, and nothing at 9, 10, 10.5 or 11px anywhere on
> screen. The stylesheet hygiene point stands; the user-facing severity does not. The fix that
> follows was scoped to the measured 3, not the declared 31.

The stylesheet declares 119 font sizes and 31 of those declarations are under 14px, with a long
tail into sizes that would not work on a phone **if they reached the screen**:

| Size | Times **declared** | Times **rendered** at 390px |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 9px | 6 | 0 |
| 10px | 5 | 0 |
| 10.5px | 1 | 0 |
| 11px | 12 | 0 |
| 12px | 6 | 0 |
| 13px | 1 | 0 |
| 13.5px | — | 3 |

**Why this is worse in Thai than the same numbers would be in English.** Thai stacks marks in four
vertical zones — upper vowels, tone marks above those, lower vowels below the baseline — so a Thai
glyph carries more information in the same em box than a Latin one. At 9-11px the tone marks stop
being distinguishable from each other and from the vowels above them: `เ ้ ็ ๊` collapse into
similar smudges. A 10px Thai face is not equivalent to 10px English; it is closer to 8px English in
readable detail. There is also no Thai typographic tradition of small caps or condensed labels to
fall back on, so small Thai text has nowhere to go but illegible.

Recommendation — a real scale, replacing the ad-hoc 119:

| Token | Mobile | Desktop | Used for |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| caption | **13px** | 13px | the smallest text allowed anywhere, and rarely |
| body-sm | **15px** | 14px | card body, helper text, list items |
| body | **16px** | 16px | default, answer options, inputs |
| lead | **18px** | 18px | intro paragraph, result summary |
| h3 | 20px | 21px | card titles |
| h2 | 24px | 28px | question prompt |
| h1 | 32px | clamp to 46px | hero and result titles |

The floor matters more than the ceiling: **nothing below 13px, and 13px only for true captions.**
Note mobile body-sm is *larger* than desktop — a phone is held closer but read in worse conditions,
and an outing is often a bright room or outdoors.

## 2. Five contrast failures, all the same mistake: light grey on ivory

> **Fixed** — see the fix record at the end of this document.

Computed against the real background (`#fefdfa`), WCAG AA needs 4.5:1 for normal text:

| Element | Colour | Ratio | Verdict |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `input::placeholder` | `#a7aea9` | **2.2** | FAIL |
| footer "PERSONALITY IS A MAP…" | `#9aa29c` | **2.57** | FAIL |
| `.card-heading small` "* จำเป็น" | `#929892` | **2.9** | FAIL |
| `.fine-print` | `#8b958f` | **3.04** | FAIL |
| `.secure-note` | `#728178` | **4.03** | marginal fail |

All five are decorative-looking greys applied to text that is not decorative — the required-field
marker and the placeholder in particular are the two pieces of text a person needs most while
filling the form. Darkening to `#5d6862` clears 4.5:1 against ivory for all of them and changes the
visual weight very little.

**Checked and found fine, contrary to first appearance:** the dark green result hero. White on
`#16362f` is 13.1:1, and the muted greens on it (`#b4c6bd`, `#9eb2a8`, `#91ac9d`) are 7.33, 5.86 and
5.36. An automated sweep first reported these as failures because it resolved the wrong background;
they were re-checked by hand against the actual colours and they pass. Recorded because the
temptation is to "fix" them and make the hero worse.

## 3. The result page is three screens long on a phone, with nothing to say so

| | Mobile 390 |
|---|---:|
| Page height | **2569px** |
| Viewport | 844px |
| Scrolling | **3.0 screens** |
| Hero card alone | 727px — **86% of the viewport** |

So a participant opens their result, sees the hero filling the screen, and gets no signal that
two-thirds of the content is below. `docs/ONSITE_ACTIVITY_50_MIN.md` allocates a **"60-sec silent
read"** to this moment. Nobody reads three screens in sixty seconds.

Two fixes, and they are different fixes:

- **Make the top screen a complete 60-second read** — character, type, and the two or three lines
  that matter — so someone who reads only that has had the intended experience.
- **Signal the rest exists.** The hero ending exactly at the fold is the worst case: it reads as the
  end of the page. Let the next section's heading intrude ~60px above the fold.

## 4. The question screen scrolls, 24 times

> **Fixed** — the page is now 844px against an 844px viewport. See the fix record.

Measured on mobile: page height **867px** against an 844px viewport. Only 23px over — but it means
the "ถัดไป" button sits below the fold by a hair on every single question, so the interaction is
answer → scroll → tap, twenty-four times.

The card itself is fine: one column at 390px, each answer 320×88px, all tap targets over 44px, the
prompt at 24px/34.8px line height. It is the 23px that ruins it.

Cheapest fix: drop the footer on the question step, or reduce the card's vertical padding from 22px
to 16px on mobile. Better fix: make the actions row sticky to the bottom of the viewport on mobile,
so the primary action never moves for 24 screens.

## 5. The stylesheet is why nobody can predict a change

> **Fixed for the hero cluster** — 51 rules replaced by one authoritative block with zero pixel
> change. The rest of the stylesheet is untouched. See the fix record.

| Selector | Times redefined |
|---|---:|
| `.welcome-grid` | 13 |
| `.hero-art` | 13 |
| `.guild-visual` | 12 |
| `.guild-logo` | 7 |
| `.guild-orbit` | 4 |

673 lines, 56 `@media` blocks. The hero's current proportions are not a decision anyone made; they
are the residue of thirteen layered overrides. This is the reason the hero "doesn't feel
proportioned" and the reason a designer's change will keep landing somewhere unexpected.

This should be consolidated before the design pass, not after — otherwise the new design gets
layered on as override fourteen.

## 6. Commitments the playbook already made that the app does not meet

`docs/ONSITE_ACTIVITY_50_MIN.md` is an approved operational spec. Five things in it have no
implementation, and they are better suggestions than anything invented from scratch because the
project already decided it wants them.

**a. Personal User Manual (playbook minutes 39–47).** Participants write, on paper:
*ฉันทำงานได้ดีเมื่อ… / เมื่อกดดันช่วยฉันโดย… / อย่าคาดเดาว่า…* The result page already generates
material very close to all three. Pre-filling those three sentences from the result, editable, and
saveable as an image would collapse a paper exercise into the product and give people something they
actually want to keep. **This is the highest-value addition on the list.**

**b. Export and delete route (playbook minutes 47–50).** The closing reflection promises a
"delete/export route". There is neither. Nothing is stored today so delete is vacuous, but export is
not — and once the January calibration write exists, delete stops being vacuous and becomes a PDPA
obligation.

**c. QR code and short URL.** The playbook's pre-event operations require both. Neither exists.

**d. Large-print path.** The playbook requires large-print cards for accessibility. The app's 9–11px
text is the opposite of that. A single "ขยายตัวอักษร" toggle that steps the whole scale up ~25%
would serve it, and would also serve anyone reading on a phone in sunlight.

**e. Offline / paper contingency.** Required by the playbook. If the venue's wifi fails, the activity
has no fallback. The app is fully client-side after first load, so a service worker would make it
survive losing the network mid-session — which is a real risk at an offsite.

## 7. Two smaller things, both cheap

> **Both fixed** — see the fix record.

**The answer buttons promise keyboard input and do not deliver.** Each option renders a letter badge
— A, B, C, D — which is a strong affordance for "press A". Pressing A does nothing. Either wire the
keys up (four lines) or drop the badges; promising an interaction and not having it is worse than
neither.

**The two bars at the bottom of the result page do not look clickable.** They are collapsible
sections rendered as flat empty bars with no chevron, no hover state and no cursor change. Most
participants will never open them, which means the content inside — the leader/HR conversation guide
— is effectively unpublished.

---

## Priority, if this is done in one pass

1. Type scale floor at 13px, and the five contrast fixes. Cheapest, biggest, affects every screen.
2. The 23px on the question screen. One line of CSS against 24 repetitions of friction.
3. Consolidate the hero/grid CSS **before** the design pass.
4. Result page: make the first screen a complete read, and signal the rest.
5. Personal User Manual on the result page.
6. Keyboard answering, and chevrons on the collapsible bars.

Items 1, 2 and 6 are code with no design decision in them. Items 3, 4 and 5 want a designer's eye
first.

## Fixed on 2026-09-10, with the measurement that proves it

Items 1, 2 and 6 from the priority list were the three with no design decision in them, plus item
3 (the CSS consolidation) which turned out to be verifiable without one. All four were done and
re-measured on the built production bundle, not the dev server.

| # | Change | Verification |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The failing greys → `#5d6862`; the three sub-14px nodes raised | 8 rendered text/placeholder nodes measured at 390px: ratios **5.38–5.80** against a 4.5:1 requirement, no failures. Rendered nodes under 14px: **3 → 2**, both now 13.5px `small` captions in the audience picker, at the review's own floor. |
| 2 | Question step: card padding 22px → 16px on mobile, progress margin 24px → 20px, footer hidden on that step | Page height **867px → 844px** against an 844px viewport: **fits exactly, no scroll**, on all 24 questions. `footer` computes to `display: none` on the question step only. |
| 3 | The hero frame cluster: **51 rules replaced by one authoritative block** | Computed styles for `.guild-visual`, `.guild-visual > img`, `.guild-orbit`, `.guild-logo`, both pseudo-elements, `.hero-art`, `.welcome-grid` and `.hero-copy` dumped at 360/390/768/1024/1440px before and after, then pixel-diffed: **0.000% of pixels differ at every width, max channel Δ 0.** The one remaining stylesheet difference is `object-fit` on the logo (`cover` → `contain`), which cannot change a pixel: the logo is 1554×1554 in a square box. |
| 6 | A/B/C/D keys answer, Enter/→ advances; chevrons, hover and `cursor: pointer` on both collapsible bars | Pressing **"C" selects 1 option**; **Enter** moves "ข้อ 1 จาก 24" → "ข้อ 2 จาก 24". Keys are ignored while focus is in an input, and with a modifier held. Both bars report `cursor: pointer` and a `::after` chevron that rotates 180° when the section opens. |

Two things found while verifying, worth recording because both were bugs in *my own* first attempt
rather than in the original code:

- Putting the logo's mobile shrink at `max-width: 899px` made the logo **34px on a 768px tablet
  where it had been 40px**. The old cluster shrank it only at ≤390px, and left its offset and
  corner radius unchanged at every width. Corrected, and the reason is written into the CSS.
- The frame's image matte needs `!important` on `inset`/`width`/`height`. next/image's `fill` sets
  those three on the element itself, so a plain rule loses. The pre-consolidation code carried the
  same `!important` for the same reason; dropping it let the picture bleed to the frame edge.

Still open from this review, all needing a designer's eye first: **§3** (result page as a 60-second
read), **§5** is now done as item 3 above, **§6a** the Personal User Manual, **§6b–e** export/QR/
large-print/offline.
