# TDFB Personality Quest — Assessment Content Audit

## Scope and review lens

This audit covers the 18 foundation questions, 5 MBTI/A–T challenge questions, 9 Enneagram core challenges, 9 generated wing challenges, their answer options, the scoring/selection context, fixtures, and the mobile question presentation. It is a content and Thai UX-writing review, not a validation study. No final rewritten items are provided.

## Executive summary: five highest-impact issues

1. **The generated `ค่อนข้าง…` options are often ungrammatical and do not form a reliable four-point scale.** Examples include `ค่อนข้างได้อยู่เงียบ ๆ กับตัวเอง`, `ค่อนข้างการอยู่กับโลกข้างใน`, and `ค่อนข้างรู้แผนหลักและเวลาสำคัญ`. The qualifier is attached to phrases of incompatible grammatical forms, so employees may compare fluency rather than preference strength.
2. **All wing challenges require respondents to understand Enneagram numbers.** Options such as `พลังของ Enneagram 4 อย่างชัดเจน` contain no motivation or lived experience. A person who does not already know the model cannot answer meaningfully; a person who does may answer toward a desired label.
3. **Several items combine multiple constructs inside one option.** Frequent `และ` pairs—such as knowledge plus private space, consistency plus fairness, people impact plus values, freedom plus hope—make it impossible to know which half motivated the answer. Explicit weights then treat the compound choice as a single signal.
4. **Timeframe and context are inconsistent or absent.** Terms such as `เรื่องใหม่`, `โจทย์`, `เมื่อเห็นต่าง`, `เมื่อถูกประเมิน`, `เวลาตึงเครียด`, and `สิ่งสำคัญ` could refer to work, private life, a rare crisis, or a typical week. Employees may answer from different contexts and produce incomparable evidence.
5. **Some alternatives carry unequal social desirability or emotional intensity.** `ถูกต้อง`, `เป็นธรรม`, `มีน้ำใจ`, `รับผิดชอบ`, and `ปกป้องคนที่พึ่งพาเรา` sound admirable, while `ควบคุม`, `ไม่มีใครต้องการ`, `ไร้ความสามารถ`, and `ไม่มีตัวตน` can sound harsh or clinical. This encourages image management, particularly in an employee setting.

## Proposed Thai writing rules for the future revision

1. **One situation per question.** Do not make respondents switch between work, private life, relationships, travel, and high-stress events within the same instrument unless the domain change is explicit.
2. **One idea per question and per option.** Avoid joining constructs with `และ`, `หรือ`, or paired outcomes unless both words name one indivisible concept. A score must have one interpretable reason.
3. **Use a clear timeframe and context.** Prefer a familiar frame such as a normal workweek, the start of a new task, a routine disagreement, or the first moments after ordinary feedback. Avoid unbounded `เมื่อ…`, `เรื่องใหม่`, `สิ่งสำคัญ`, and `เวลาตึงเครียด`.
4. **Keep options neutral, parallel, and similarly attractive.** Use the same grammatical form, comparable length, comparable emotional intensity, and the same level of abstraction. Avoid making one option sound mature, kind, responsible, heroic, controlling, needy, or deficient.
5. **Ask motivation rather than visible behaviour.** When behaviour is used as a situation, ask what the person is trying to protect, gain, or resolve. Do not infer motivation from an action that can arise for several reasons.
6. **Use `ข้อใดใกล้กับคุณที่สุด` where appropriate.** This supports a best-fit choice without claiming absolutes. Add the relevant context in the question rather than relying only on the repeated UI instruction.
7. **Do not prefix arbitrary phrases with `ค่อนข้าง`.** If degree is needed, design a grammatically stable response scale separate from the statement, or write four natural and distinct alternatives.
8. **Hide assessment labels and scoring direction.** Do not show `Enneagram 1–9`, `A/T`, poles, or type names inside answer options. Every employee should be able to answer without prior model knowledge.
9. **Prefer plain, literal Thai.** Review metaphors such as `ได้ยินความคิด`, `เสียงข้างใน`, `เข็มทิศ`, `พาตัวเองผ่านไป`, and `แรงเสริม`; retain only those verified as immediately understandable in employee testing.
10. **Keep mobile scan length predictable.** Aim for one short question and four options of roughly equal length, ideally one to two lines each on a narrow phone. Avoid one option becoming conspicuously longer or conceptually denser.
11. **Avoid absolutes and clinical intensity.** Use `โดยทั่วไป`, `มัก`, or a bounded situation instead of `มากที่สุด` when the construct does not require an extreme. Replace identity-threatening or diagnostic-sounding language with ordinary reflection language.
12. **Content-test independently of scoring.** Before changing weights, conduct comprehension interviews to verify that employees paraphrase each option as the intended motivation and can explain why alternatives are distinct.

## UI observations affecting content comprehension

- The UI consistently shows one question and four large answer buttons, which supports mobile focus.
- The repeated helper `เลือกคำตอบที่ใกล้เคียงคุณที่สุด` is useful, but it cannot resolve missing context or compound choices inside the item.
- Letter labels A–D and check marks make selection clear; they do not indicate a scale, so generated strong/moderate endpoint logic is invisible to respondents.
- Challenge items use generic context labels (`คำถามแยกแนวโน้ม`, `คำถามแยกแรงขับ`, `คำถามแยกปีก`). `คำถามแยกปีก` is assessment jargon and may prime model-aware respondents.
- The progress and remaining-time copy encourages quick reading, increasing the impact of long, abstract, or metaphorical wording.

## Content-review limitations

This desk review identifies wording and construct-risk hypotheses. It does not establish reliability, validity, fairness, response-process evidence, or psychological interpretation. A future revision requires expert construct review plus Thai employee comprehension testing before any validation claim.
